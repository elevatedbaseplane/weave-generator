## Batch size: complete behaviors rather than feature counts

The 3–5 addition guideline is not a ceiling or quota. Combine small controls, display, inspector, serialization and tests that deliver one coherent behavior. Do not create a release for every checkbox. Keep geometry kernels, new analytical semantics, migrations and receiver integrations bounded even if they expose only one feature.

Retain the existing batch IDs as tracking units. Several may be completed in one explicitly approved delivery if:
1. Their dependencies can be fulfilled in sequence within that scope.
2. Their semantic decisions are already settled.
3. The combined result is reviewable with a coherent automated suite and at most five useful visual acceptance steps.
4. Save/restore, failure handling and source compatibility are completed, not postponed.
5. The builder can diagnose failure without mixing several unresolved algorithms.

Reasonable consolidation candidates: R0A–R0C in one planning engagement; R4A+R4B once the vocabulary is settled; R8A+R8B if receiver access and compatibility are already established. Consolidation is a scope proposal, never permission to skip an entry's exit. Do not default to combining R1D's field composition with R2A's intersection kernel, R5 intensity with R6 significance, R7 synthesis with R9 radii, or R11 topology with a broad regional vocabulary.

Split an entry further if it contains multiple unresolved operations. R10's paired advanced operations may need separate deliveries after their contracts are examined. Neither 63 mandatory releases nor a promised completion time follows from this checklist.
