## Definition and recurring logic

Weave Generator is the first analytical and generative field in the larger B.A.C. workflow: a relational field generator and analytical synthesis tool. It creates controlled systems of interacting lines, identifies and visually encodes their relationships, extracts significant conditions, and synthesizes them into geometry and metadata that influence subsequent design stages.

Its recurring logic is: a field of interactions is analyzed; relationships are made legible; significant conditions are selected; those conditions are synthesized into a part capable of entering another field.

Lines act within the system, but the primary subject is the relationship between them and the collective conditions those relationships produce. Authored forces and constraints create controlled emergence. Generation must not be optimized toward a predetermined outline simply to make downstream export convenient. The supplied reference to Combinatory Urbanism describes the user's conceptual translation; no additional claims about the book are established here.

## Governing workflow

Authored boundary, carriers, constraints and influences → interacting line field → relational classifications and degrees of intensity → stitch-based analytical language → selected significant points → synthesized closed polylines with relational metadata → Tangent formal differentiation → Overlap relationships and residual spaces.

Analysis and synthesis are both core outputs. The analytical drawing remains independently useful and exportable. A polyline compresses selected relational behavior into a coherent geometric organization; it need not trace the threads, a seam, or every observed condition.

## Required conceptual capabilities

- Generate comparable variations through deliberate controls for carriers, directions, density, deformation, tension and influences. Preserve reproducibility and source identity.
- Observe relationships including convergence/divergence, proximity, crossings, alignment, parallelism, repetition, clustering, density, isolation, interruption, continuity, directional reinforcement and rhythm changes. Collision and instability require definitions; their names do not establish physical simulation.
- Describe both condition type and strength. Relational intensity can eventually combine several measurements, but its components, normalization, neighborhood scale and weights must remain inspectable. Do not reduce every reading to an unexplained universal score.
- Distinguish authored influences, which drive generation, from derived relational-intensity fields, which describe results. Feedback from analysis into generation is a separate future decision, not an implicit loop.
- Use stitch patterns as a visual taxonomy of relationships and intensity. Markings are analytical records, independently visible/exportable, rather than implicit binding or rerouting operations.
- Extract points because significant relationships or transitions occur there. Record evidence, type, intensity components, directions, neighboring relationships, classifications, source revision and extraction rule. Significance includes low-interaction or isolated conditions when selected by the designer; it is not synonymous with highest density.
- Synthesize selected moments into closed polylines through explicit relational choices. Record why vertices and connections contribute, including authored choices. Seam tracing remains one optional strategy and analytical view, not the governing definition of composition.
- Export geometry and relational information. Define lineage and intended radius behavior early enough that information is not lost during synthesis. Radius guidance is a core downstream objective, even if implementation is staged.
- Support iteration, comparison, selection, rejection, overrides, locking and manual intervention. Keep measured evidence distinct from interpretation and preserve the designer's responsibility for judgment.
- Eventually reuse a deliberately designed downstream spatial boundary for a secondary/internal weave. This second role explores internal organization rather than automatically determining the boundary again. Circulation, occupation, partitions, structure, visibility, furniture or sectional readings require appropriate authored context and later contracts.
- Allow the relational vocabulary to recur at larger architectural scales without assuming that a line-scale measurement alone proves a spatial or social outcome.

## What this update changes

1. Relationship and collective organization replace the narrow compressed-seam example as the organizing subject.
2. Relational intensity and gradients become explicit requirements, alongside categorical detection.
3. Significant-point selection is broader than interface endpoints and bends.
4. Closed-polyline synthesis becomes a primary conceptual act. No rule restricts it to tracing boundaries, closing relation cycles, or outlining supporting threads; future synthesis rules must instead justify their choices and validate geometry.
5. Downstream metadata and radius influence are planned core behavior, not incidental export enhancements. Receiver support remains unimplemented and must be verified separately.
6. The secondary/internal-weave role is retained as a later use of the same relational logic.

The compressed-seam specification is retained as a possible detector fixture. Its compression-first ordering, numerical defaults, endpoint extraction, band-outline construction and C1–C3 Sol handoff are provisional and no longer the governing next build. Do not discard its useful test ideas or implement it unchanged as the master strategy.
